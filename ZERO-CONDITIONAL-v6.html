Discover: rule or one-off? 5 min

Big idea: use the zero conditional in reports for facts, rules and routines that are always true: “If this happens, that happens.” It is not for single events or predictions.

Learn the formula 6 min

From the report: If pupils complete their homework, they usually make better progress.

If+present simple,present simple
PartWhat to useExample
Condition (if-clause)present simpleIf pupils complete their homework,
Resultpresent simple (no will)they usually make better progress.
Singular subject (he/she/it)verb + sIf a pupil feels unwell, the nurse checks them.
Plural subject (they)base verbIf pupils want a lighter lunch, they choose the salad bar.
Result firstno commaPupils make better progress if they complete their homework.
Mistake to avoid: ✗ If it rains, pupils will stay in the hall. ✓ If it rains, pupils stay in the hall.
Tips: when can replace if: “When lunchtime begins, pupils walk to the canteen.” Words like usually, often and most make a report sound accurate.

Guided practice: choose the correct forms 7 min

Each sentence belongs to a different paragraph of the school report. Choose the pair of verbs that completes it, then press Check.

Independent task: join the sentences 6 min

Join each pair into one zero conditional sentence for the matching paragraph of the report. Use present simple in both parts.
Challenge: Choose one paragraph of your own report (Learning Experiences, The School Environment, Break Times, School Meals, Staff and Support or Why School Matters) and write one zero conditional sentence for it. Add a signpost such as In addition or As a result.
